## Bayesian Experimental Design

Bayesian experimental design (BED) is a decision-theoretic framework for choosing experiments that are expected to be most informative about an unknown parameter $\theta_0$. To formalize this, we introduce a random variable $\theta$ and encode our initial beliefs about $\theta_0$ in a prior distribution $p(\theta)$. An experiment is assumed to generate an observable outcome $y$ according to a likelihood $p(y \mid \theta, \xi)$, where $\xi$ represents the controllable factors of the experiment, i.e. its *design*. 

After observing an outcome $y$, we update our beliefs about $\theta_0$ using Bayes' rule to obtain the posterior distribution:

$$
p(\theta \mid y, \xi) = \frac{p(y \mid \theta, \xi)\, p(\theta)}{p(y \mid \xi)},
$$

where $p(y \mid \xi) = \int p(y \mid \theta, \xi)\, p(\theta)\, \mathrm{d}\theta$ denotes the marginal likelihood, and we have assumed that the prior does not depend on the design, $p(\theta \mid \xi) = p(\theta)$. Provided that sufficient regularity conditions hold, $\theta_0$ lies in the support of the prior, and our model is well-specified (more on this later), the posterior concentrates around $\theta_0$ as we observe more outcomes.

Since different designs produce different outcome distributions and hence different posteriors, we would like to choose $\xi$ so that the posterior concentrates as efficiently as possible. One way to quantify this concentration is with the entropy of the posterior, which brings us to the classic objective function of BED: *expected information gain* (EIG).
