Which experiments reveal the most information about a hidden variable? In this post, I will give an informal introduction to Bayesian experimental design (BED) and discuss some recent methods for making it adaptive. Of particular interest is the case where our assumed simulator of outcomes is *misspecified*, so that the experiments that appear most informative under this simulator need not be informative in the true environment. Can we hedge against this possibility, and if so, at what cost to identification when the simulator is in fact correct?

The material draws on my MSc dissertation and on subsequent research. Code for the plots can be found in the GitHub [repository](https://github.com/nicholasjroy/MSc-Dissertation).

## Bayesian Experimental Design

At its core, BED offers principled criteria for choosing actions or experimental *designs* that maximally reduce our uncertainty (in expectation) about a fixed but unknown parameter $$ \theta^* $$. Since $$ \theta^* $$ is unknown, we introduce a random variable $$ \theta $$ in its place and encode our beliefs about its value in a prior $$ p(\theta) $$. The *experiment* is assumed to generate an observable outcome $$ y $$ that depends on $$ \theta^* $$ and our chosen design $$ \xi $$ according to the likelihood $$ p(y \mid \theta^*, \xi) $$. We can then use this outcome to update our beliefs about $$ \theta^* $$ via Bayes' rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(\theta \mid y, \xi) &= \frac{p(y \mid \theta, \xi)\, p(\theta)}{p(y \mid \xi)}, \\[4pt]
\text{where} \quad p(y \mid \xi) &:= \int p(y \mid \theta, \xi)\, p(\theta)\, \mathrm{d}\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$ 

In other words, the posterior upweights values of $$ \theta $$ under which the observed outcome $$y$$ is more likely. Assuming the likelihood is well-specified and $$ \theta^* $$ lies within the support of our prior, the posterior tends to concentrate around the true value $$ \theta^* $$ as observations accumulate ([van der Vaart, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511802256)). The interesting question is then: which designs $$ \xi $$ lead to the greatest concentration of our beliefs around the truth—that is, which are most informative?
