Which actions reveal the most information about a hidden variable? And can we trust those actions when our notion of "informativeness" itself depends on imperfect beliefs? To address these questions, I aim to provide an informal introduction to the framework of Bayesian experimental design (BED) and its recent evolution under deep learning. This draws partly on research I conducted for my MSc dissertation, but I hope to broaden the discussion toward the end with connections to world models. Code for the experiments is available in the GitHub [repository](https://github.com/nicholasjroy/MSc-Dissertation).

## Bayesian experimental design

At its core, BED offers principled decision criteria for choosing actions or experimental *designs* that maximally reduce our uncertainty about a fixed but unknown parameter $$ \theta^* $$. Since $$ \theta^* $$ is unknown, we introduce a random variable $$ \theta $$ in its place and encode our beliefs about its value in a prior $$ p(\theta) $$. The *experiment* is assumed to generate an observable outcome $$ y $$ that depends on $$ \theta^* $$ and our chosen design $$ \xi $$ according to the likelihood $$ p(y \mid \theta^*, \xi) $$. We then use this outcome to update our beliefs about $$ \theta^* $$ using Bayes' rule:

$$
p(\theta \mid y, \xi) = \frac{p(y \mid \theta, \xi)\, p(\theta)}{p(y \mid \xi)}, \qquad \text{where} \;\; p(y \mid \xi) := \int p(y \mid \theta, \xi)\, p(\theta)\, \mathrm{d}\theta.
$$ 

In other words, the posterior upweights values of $$ \theta $$ in the support of the prior for which the observed outcome $$ y $$ has higher likelihood. Assuming the likelihood is well-specified and $$ \theta^* $$ lies within the support of our prior, the posterior tends to concentrate around the true value $$ \theta^* $$ ([Kleijn & van der Vaart, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1214/12-EJS675)). The interesting question is then: which design $$ \xi $$ leads to the greatest concentration of our beliefs around the truth—that is, which is most informative?
